The data supports microbiome impact on metabolic health

Three lines of published evidence that informed every ingredient choice in AfterSlim.

Twin study

Gut bacteria, weight, and the link beyond genetics

Transplanting the microbiome from an obese twin into germ-free mice drove obesity. Transplanting from the lean twin drove leanness. Same genetics, different microbiome, opposite outcomes.

Ridaura VK, et al. Gut microbiota from twins discordant for obesity modulate metabolism in mice. Science, 2013.

GLP-1 stimulation

Akkermansia secretes a protein that stimulates GLP-1 production

The P9 protein produced by Akkermansia muciniphila stimulates intestinal L-cells to release GLP-1 naturally. Human intestinal cell models confirm the effect.

+ GLP-1

Yoon HS, et al. Akkermansia muciniphila secretes a glucagon-like peptide-1-inducing protein that improves glucose homeostasis. Nature Microbiology, 2021.

Mucus layer repair

Akkermansia strengthens the intestinal barrier

Daily Akkermansia supplementation in overweight and insulin-resistant humans improved insulin sensitivity, reduced inflammatory markers, and reinforced the gut barrier.

+30%

Depommier C, et al. Supplementation with Akkermansia muciniphila in overweight and obese human volunteers. Nature Medicine, 2019.

Clostridium butyricum increases butyrate

Butyrate is the short-chain fatty acid that colon cells use as their primary energy source. More butyrate means a stronger gut barrier, less inflammation, and steadier satiety signaling between meals.

By pairing Clostridium butyricum with resistant starch (its preferred substrate), AfterSlim creates a continuous fermentation cycle in the colon, not a one-time delivery.

Stoeva MK, et al. Butyrate-producing human gut symbiont Clostridium butyricum and its role in health and disease. Gut Microbes, 2021.
