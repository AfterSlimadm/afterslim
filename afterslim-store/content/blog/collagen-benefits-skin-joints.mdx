## What Is Collagen?

Collagen is the most abundant protein in the human body, accounting for roughly **30% of total protein content**. It provides structural support to your skin, bones, tendons, ligaments, and connective tissues. Think of it as the scaffolding that holds everything together.

Starting in your mid-twenties, your body's natural collagen production begins to decline at a rate of about 1-1.5% per year. By the time you reach your forties, the cumulative loss becomes visible: fine lines appear, joints feel stiffer, and hair may lose some of its thickness. Supplementing with collagen peptides can help replenish what time takes away.

## Understanding Collagen Types

Not all collagen is the same. Scientists have identified at least 28 types, but five are most relevant for supplementation:

### Type I

The most prevalent type in the human body, Type I collagen is found in skin, bones, tendons, and organs. It is the primary driver of **skin elasticity** and firmness. Studies show that supplementing with Type I collagen peptides can improve skin hydration and reduce the appearance of wrinkles within eight to twelve weeks.

### Type II

Type II collagen is concentrated in cartilage and is essential for **joint health**. It cushions your joints and helps them move smoothly. People dealing with occasional joint stiffness or those who exercise regularly may benefit from Type II supplementation.

### Type III

Often found alongside Type I, Type III collagen supports the structure of blood vessels, muscles, and internal organs. It plays a role in gut lining integrity and cardiovascular health.

### Type V

Type V collagen is a lesser-known but important type that contributes to the formation of cell membranes and the tissue surrounding developing babies during pregnancy. It also works synergistically with Types I and III to support skin and hair quality.

### Type X

Type X collagen is involved in bone formation and cartilage mineralization. It supports healthy bone development and may play a role in joint repair processes.

## Proven Benefits of Collagen Supplementation

### Skin Elasticity and Hydration

A growing body of clinical research supports collagen peptides for skin health. One meta-analysis of 19 studies found that participants who took hydrolyzed collagen for 90 days experienced significant improvements in skin elasticity, hydration, and wrinkle depth compared to placebo groups.

### Joint Comfort and Mobility

For active individuals and aging adults alike, collagen can help maintain joint flexibility. Type II collagen, in particular, has been shown to reduce joint discomfort and improve range of motion. Athletes who supplemented with 10 grams of collagen peptides daily reported less activity-related joint pain after 24 weeks.

### Hair and Nail Strength

Collagen provides the amino acids -- especially **proline** and **glycine** -- that your body uses to build keratin, the protein that forms hair and nails. Many users report thicker hair and stronger, less brittle nails after consistent supplementation.

### Gut Lining Support

The amino acid glutamine, found in collagen, helps maintain the integrity of the intestinal lining. This benefit is particularly relevant for individuals interested in supporting overall digestive wellness.

- **Pair with vitamin C** -- Your body needs vitamin C to synthesize collagen. Include citrus fruits, bell peppers, or a vitamin C supplement in your routine.
